# LC 121 · Best Time to Buy & Sell Stock
Given prices[], find max profit from one buy+sell.
Must buy before sell. Return 0 if no profit possible.

Constraints: 1 ≤ n ≤ 10⁵  ·  0 ≤ prices[i] ≤ 10⁴

// interview Q&A — click to reveal

  


    

      
Q1 — Why does greedy work here?

      
One transaction means: for any sell day, the best buy is the cheapest day before it. Tracking the running minimum ensures we never miss it — no look-back needed.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q2 — Is this secretly DP?

      
Yes — exactly right to say so. Two DP states: minPrice and maxProfit. Rolling them in-place gives O(1) space without losing the recurrence insight. Say this in interviews.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q3 — Unlimited transactions? (LC 122)

      
Sum every positive consecutive difference. You capture every upward move independently. Equivalent to buying every local min and selling every local max in one pass.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q4 — At most 2 transactions? (LC 123)

      
4 variables: buy1, sell1, buy2, sell2. Process each price updating all four. Or DP with state (hold/not) × (txn count). O(n) time O(1) space.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q5 — At most k transactions? (LC 188)

      
Generalize to 2k variables. When k ≥ n/2, reduce to the unlimited case — you can't use more trades than pairs of days. O(kn) otherwise.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q6 — Cooldown after sell? (LC 309)

      
3 DP states: held, sold, rested. Transitions: rested = max(rested, sold), sold = held + price, held = max(held, rested − price). One pass, O(1) space.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q7 — Transaction fee? (LC 714)

      
Two states: cash (not holding), hold (holding). cash[i] = max(cash, hold + price − fee), hold[i] = max(hold, cash − price). Subtract fee on sell only.

      ▶ expand
    


    

      
Q8 — How do you test this?

      
[7,1,5,3,6,4]→5, [7,6,4,3,1]→0, [1,2,3,4,5]→4, [5]→0, [2,1]→0, [1,2]→1. Cover: normal, all-down, monotone-up, single, two-element both directions.
