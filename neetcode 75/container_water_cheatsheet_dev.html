# LC 11 · Container With Most Water
Given heights[], find two lines that together with x-axis
form a container holding the most water. Return max area.

area = min(heights[l], heights[r]) × (r − l)
Constraints: 2 ≤ n ≤ 10⁵  ·  0 ≤ heights[i] ≤ 10⁴

✓ Pattern: Two Pointers (shrink from both ends)

// interview Q&A — click to reveal




  

    
Q1 — Why move the shorter pointer?

    
Area = min(h[l],h[r]) × width. If we move the taller side, width decreases and height is still capped by the shorter bar — so area can only stay same or drop. Moving the shorter side is the only way to possibly find a taller bar that compensates.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q2 — Does this miss any pair?

    
No. When we discard a pointer position, every pair with that position as the shorter side has already been considered (we computed area at max width). Formal proof: by contradiction — any skipped pair would have smaller area than something we already tracked.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q3 — What's wrong with Integer.MIN_VALUE init?

    
Area is always ≥ 0, so Integer.MIN_VALUE is misleading — it implies area can be negative. Use 0. Also: the redundant left < right inside the if-block is already guaranteed by the while condition — remove it.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q4 — How is this different from Trapping Rain Water?

    
Container (LC 11): one pair of lines, area between them, no bars in between matter. Trapping Rain Water (LC 42): every bar matters, water trapped above each bar is summed. Different formula and approach — rain water uses prefix max arrays or a monotonic stack.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q5 — Can you do better than O(n)?

    
No — you must read every element at least once to know if it could be the optimal shorter bar. Lower bound is Ω(n). The two-pointer solution is optimal.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q6 — What if heights can be negative?

    
The problem guarantees heights ≥ 0. But if negative were allowed: area = min(h[l],h[r]) × (r-l) could be negative. The greedy still works since moving the less-optimal pointer is the right choice regardless of sign.

    ▶ expand
  


  

    
Q7 — Follow-up: maximise volume in 3D?

    
This becomes a much harder problem — equivalent to finding a box. No known O(n) solution. Generally requires O(n²) or O(n² log n) approaches. Good sign if interviewer asks this — acknowledge the increase in complexity.
